Thannitus

written by

Wyatt Newbill

FADE IN: EXT. FOREST - DUSK

Tall trees sway in the breeze as cicadas chirp and birds sing. Hold for a beat as we SUPERIMPOSE TITLE.

CUT TO:

INT. BEDROOM (SINGLE ROOM) - AFTERNOON

A SHELF of neatly labeled CASETTE TAPES: CEMETERY, ABANDONED HOUSE, STORM 05/12, BRIDGE, MEADOW. Clippings on the wall: "SCIENTISTS RECORD 'GHOST FOREST,'" an article about banshees. PHOTOS too: an enormous OLD TREE, broken branches, nature.

NARRATOR (V.O.)

Throughout history, humans have believed death announces itself before it arrives.

A NOTEBOOK lies open on a DESK, filled with recordings: dates, locations, observations. The heading of this page: DEATH ANOMALIES.

NARRATOR (V.O.) (CONT'D)

Across cultures, stories of death are often accompanied by stories of warning - voices, cries, bells, dreams. Whether coincidence or pattern depends largely on who's listening.

CORIN (19) sits at the desk, watching a documentary about death on his COMPUTER. A RECORDER sits atop a BOOK.

NARRATOR (V.O.) (CONT'D)

While not supported by evidence, these claims persist with remarkable consistency across history -

The tab is closed by Corin. The computer switches to the next tab, a page on death rattles. Corin snaps the computer shut.

SMASH CUT TO:

INT. PARKING GARAGE - NIGHT

Corin walks through the concrete structure, notebook and recorder in hand, HEADPHONES over his ears. Water drips somewhere unseen as the fluorescent tubes buzz overhead and his shoes scuff against concrete.

He stops in the middle of the level, pressing RECORD. A red light blinks on as he holds the recorder up and still.

Silence, but for the hum.

He starts to jot the time and location down in his notebook. But then - a CREAK through the headphones, low and wrong. Wood bending, somewhere wood has no business being.

Corin pulls one headphone away from his ear - just the garage, hum, drip. Presses it back - the CREAK again, folded into the sounds of the garage.

The recording continues. A low hint of sound after. Then silence. Corin lowers the recorder. Smiles - interesting. He presses PLAY again -

CUT TO:

INT. BEDROOM - DEAD OF NIGHT

Corin is in his bed, staring up at the ceiling. He tosses and turns, then settles. He closes his eyes, the only sound his breathing.

CREAK.

His eyes fly open. He listens for a tense moment, but - nothing. He exhales. Then - the frantic flutter of BIRDS taking flight overhead. A SNAP. He sits up.

CUT TO:

INT. BEDROOM - MOMENTS LATER

Corin sits at his desk, HEADPHONES on. The waveform of the recording fills the screen. He isolates the SOUND and plays it.

Only the CREAK.

He adjusts a slider - plays it again. CREAK. A vague SNAP. The faintest smile begins to grow on Corin's face.

He increases the gain. Plays it again. CREAK. SNAP. A low, wooden GROAN beneath the concrete hum. His breathing quickens as he scribbles in the notebook: ANOMALY CONFIRMED.

He leans in closer to the screen, entranced. He reverses it, plays it sped up. For the first time, it isn't a dead end. It's evidence.

His fingers dance across the keyboard as he plays the whole thing one more time. Audio crackles: CREAK - SNAP - GROAN - a faint, human INHALE.

Corin freezes, staring at the screen. He rewinds. Listens again. It's still there.

He leans back. Looks up from the screen, eyes searching the room. He slowly removes the headphones.

A grin flickers across his face, equal parts awe and disbelief. He laughs out loud, presses PLAY one final time. Now, the inhale resolves into a desperate, involuntary GASP.

Corin's grin fades slightly. He slowly reaches for the recorder, grabbing it and pressing RECORD. He leans towards it, takes a slow breath in. Stops.

He plays it back - his own inhale - and overlays it with the recording. The rhythms align. Not perfectly, but close enough for it to be uncomfortable.

Corin does the same with the gasp - same result. Recognition slowly overtakes wonder.

CORIN

(whispered)

... It's me.

The SOUND is heard again, crescendoing. CREAK - SNAP - GROAN - GASP -

SMASH CUT TO:

EXT. VARIOUS LOCATIONS - DAY

A montage, brisk and quiet. Corin records an ALLEYWAY, recorder held up to a dumpster's shadow. An EMPTY STAIRWELL. A brisk walk between BUILDINGS.

At each stop, he checks a COMPASS, jots the time and direction in his notebook. The recordings differ in place, but under each one is that same SOUND.

CUT TO:

INT. BEDROOM - EVENING

MAPS now litter the desk, marked with arrows converging on a single point in the FOREST. The notebook is open next to a bowl of DINNER. Under the heading THE SOUND is a long list of his recordings: 4:18 PM NW, 5:42 PM NW, 6:03 PM NW.

He rewinds the SOUND and plays it, then makes a note in his notebook, grabbing the FORK as he does so. It touches the bowl - TING.

But for the briefest instant, it sounds a little like the CREAK. Corin stops. Listens - nothing.

He turns back to the computer and rewinds the SOUND, playing it again and making a note in his notebook. Plays - notes. Again and again.

CUT TO:

INT. BEDROOM - MORNING

Gray morning light spills into the room. Corin sits up sharply, startled by a sound - the hallway, the wall, maybe nowhere.

The AC clicks on - and for a split second, Corin hears the GROAN. His head whips around. Nothing.

But he doesn't even startle. The AC clicks on - and for a split second, you hear the click - but then, it's the GROAN. Nothing.

CUT TO:

EXT. SIDEWALK - MOMENTS LATER

Corin walks on campus. For the first time in a while, he seems completely normal, backpack slung over a shoulder.

But then - a sprinkler clicks on. CRACK. It's the same sound as creaking wood. Corin flinches, glances over. Ahead of him, a STUDENT drops a BOOK onto the ground. THUMP. The same sound as a falling tree. Nobody else reacts. But Corin does.

Now the audience starts noticing something:

Every sound has the same texture. A bird chirping. A car braking. Shoes scuffing concrete. All of them contain pieces of the SOUND.

He reaches the CROSSWALK, where a STRANGER waits.

CORIN

Did you hear that?

STRANGER

Hear what?

Corin opens his mouth to answer and then realizes there isn't a sentence for it. There isn't a way to describe it that won't sound like what it is.

CORIN

...Nothing. Nevermind.

BEEP. The walk signal.

BEEP - INHALE. The SOUND again.

Corin stares blankly across the road, students streaming past him. His eyes drift towards the tree line unconsciously.

He pulls his notebook out of his backpack almost absentmindedly. The signal begins flashing: DON'T WALK. He makes a decision. And turns.

CUT TO:

EXT. FOREST - MOMENTS LATER

The same shot from the beginning of the movie. PAN DOWN to reveal Corin trudging through the forest, trancelike.

It's almost silent - the only things that can be heard are the sounds of the forest muffled by that soft SOUND.

Leaves snap and crackle as he stops. A bird suddenly takes flight from a nearby branch, joining a flock above. Corin doesn't look.

A low GROAN echoes somewhere above him. He keeps walking. A few leaves fall around him. Still nothing. A CRACK, ever so softly.

CUT TO:

EXT. CLEARING - MOMENTS LATER

Corin reaches a clearing, where a massive, aging tree - the old tree pinned to his wall - stands at the center.

He walks towards the tree and stops. There's a BROKEN BRANCH on the ground.

For the first time in the film, he looks peaceful. The forest is quiet. The SOUND is quiet. Almost gone.

He takes a step forward. Then - a small CRACK. The tree shifts. Corin doesn't hear it.

PAN UP to the BRANCH - another CRACK. The SOUND. The very one he's been chasing. But now it is indistinguishable from everything else. Another CRACK -

Corin stands under the branch, looking up - not afraid, but almost... fulfilled. The sound disappears. For the first time in the movie, there is absolute silence.

Corin subtly smiles.

CUT TO BLACK.

The sound is played on full blast.

After the gasp at the end is silence - and then, one final epilogue. Birds FLOCKING, scattering into the open sky.

END.
