Every so often a man will rise above the fray of everyday life and alter the meandering path of history. It is ever so slightly, often no more than a rut or a divot, but nevertheless enough to leave their mark. These men are few and far in between, and yet their actions hold the most significance out of all of humankind — an interesting quirk of humanity. More importantly, these men often don’t hold significant power — they aren’t kings, queens, presidents, senators, businessmen. They are ordinary people who have the mantle of leadership thrust onto them by their predecessors, whether through laziness, corruption, or something of the like. They are the diamonds in the dirt, forged by society into something that will not break or shatter. But for one diamond, there are a thousand zircons. And sadly, more often than not men who believe themselves to be the diamonds take power for themselves.

This is not a story of diamonds, nor of zircons. It is a story of one man, one who did not rise above the fray, one who stayed with the herd. A normal man who had the privilege to witness power in its ugliest and most beautiful form. It is a story of loss and betrayal. It is a story of both pain, and power.

He had lived for years under the oppressive regime known to the people as the Dominion. I could tell you its official and bureaucratic name, but it’s much too long for a simple story like this, and what’s more, you wouldn’t understand it at all. Simpler to know it as the Dominion. The Dominion had been established many years ago, when the man was a child, around the age of 5. He vaguely remembered through the haze of memory fevered speeches and violent protests in his childhood, but that was only on the rare occasions where he had too much to drink and his mind turned to the past. He was, in most aspects, a model Dominion citizen, albeit a strange one. His habits were, to say the least, odd.
