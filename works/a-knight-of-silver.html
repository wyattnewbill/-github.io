Swords clashed over a field of grass, conducting a symphony of steel punctuated only by gasps of air. Two armored knights met on the field of battle, faces sweaty beneath silvered helms. Swordfighting is, at its essence, no more than two men returning for a time to the moments of their childhood when they would duel with sticks; parry, thrust, counterthrust, riposte.

And eventually, as all things must, the battle came to an end. With an adroit flick of the wrist, one knight—we shall call him the Silver Knight, for his armor was of gleaming silver—disarmed the other, and ended his life with a twist and a shove into his visor. The Silver Knight stood there for a time, contemplating, perhaps in shock. After all, knighthood was little more than men clad in metal striking at each other with sharp sticks. It was little more than the ability to die in the dirt slightly better than the common man.

So, as the Silver Knight yanked his sword out of the dead man’s visor, and as blood and gore trailed out with it, he doubled over, ripped off his helmet, and threw up. The smell of half-digested mutton, coppery blood, and sweat filled the air. The Silver Knight panted, hands on his knees, and retched once more before collapsing onto his back, exhausted.

His rest was interrupted by his finely built steed, Morel. Morel had ambled towards the two knights, and had begun to sniff the dead body—as one would, if one were a horse. The reek of horse and manure filled the air, and the Silver Knight rose unsteadily before yelling at him.

“Fuck thee! Thou shit-stained knave!”

The horse neighed in response, before snuffling the body. His tongue darted out and tasted the armor—the Silver Knight winced—before putting his nose under the corpse and flipping it over.

The smell grew more potent. The Silver Knight stared at the dead man—a muscle tightened in his cheek—before sheathing his sword. There was work to be done yet.
