Today I decided to walk down to the beach. And when I reached the bottom of the hill on which my house stands, I saw a sunset. A beautiful sunset, one unmatched by any I had seen before. Not in its beauty—for I have seen sunsets every day of my life since I was born—but in its very nature.

I stared at that sunset, mesmerized by its simplicity. I live in a house from which the sunset is visible every day of every year. And yet I had never seen something this beautiful.

There are, no doubt, vistas and views more awe-inspiring, more sublime, than this simple sunset. On Earth, one might conjure up views from mountaintops and hilltops, meadows and glassy lakes. One might also conjure up a Lunar earthrise, the diamond rains of the Gas Giants, the methane lakes and amber mountains of Titan, the ice-encrusted jewels that ring Saturn, the reddish plateaus of Mars. There are, most certainly, more beautiful views in a universe of infinite beauty and infinite pleasure.

But this sunset was beautiful precisely because of its simplicity—because of its commonality. There will be a million more sunsets like this one—perhaps not the exact shade of pastel, but a sunset so achingly close to this one. But this one is beautiful nonetheless. Everything pales in comparison. Everything is silhouetted against this sky, the blues and oranges, pinks, purples, reds, mixing together to form the most beautiful painting of all. The lap of the ocean waves against white sands, the feeling of the sea breeze on your face as you gaze upwards at the show that God deigned for mortal eyes to see—the feeling of freedom and independence, unshackled from the expectations of modernity.

All this and more, contained within that simple sunset.

I gazed at that sunset, wondering what it meant to be a human. What it meant to exist in this world. I pondered that idea on the walk back home, and when I turned my back on that sunset, it felt like I turned my back on something else, too.
